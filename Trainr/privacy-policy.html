Privacy Policy for Trainr

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Trainr is a workout planning app. This policy explains what it collects, where that goes, and what you can do about it. It is written to be read, not to cover anybody in legalese. It covers both the Android and the iOS app, which behave the same way and are noted separately only where they differ.

The terms you agree to by using the app are in the terms of use.

What Trainr asks you for

When you set up a profile, Trainr asks for:

  • Your preferred name
  • Your age
  • Your gender
  • Your height and weight
  • Your fitness goal, training style and experience level
  • Where you train and what equipment you have
  • How many days a week you train, how long a session is, and when you prefer to train
  • Any injuries or areas you want to work around

As you train, Trainr also records the sets, reps, weights and times you log.

Where it is stored

All of it is stored on your device, in the app’s own database. Trainr has no accounts, no server of its own, and no copy of your profile anywhere else. If you uninstall the app, that data is deleted with it.

What is sent off your device, and to whom

Nothing from your profile or your training. Your weekly plan is written by the app itself, on your device, from an exercise catalog that ships inside it. None of the answers above, and nothing you log, is sent anywhere to write it.

Adjust today (version 2.0 and later)

From version 2.0 a workout can be changed on the day, from the session screen. Four of the reasons it offers (less time, missing equipment, show me how, something hurts) are handled by the app’s own logic, on your device, like the rest of the plan. The fifth lets you type what is going on in your own words. That note is read by a small language model that runs entirely on your device (LiquidAI LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct), and what the model understood is shown back to you before anything in your plan changes. The note is not sent anywhere, and no server of ours is involved.

The model is downloaded once, the first time you choose that option, from LiquidAI’s public repository on Hugging Face (about 730 MB). That download is an ordinary web request: Hugging Face sees your IP address and the request for the file, nothing about you or your training, under the Hugging Face Privacy Policy. The file is checked against a known checksum before it is used and kept in the app’s own storage; deleting the app deletes it. A phone without room for the download is told so, and the other four reasons keep working without it.

The exercise videos

Tutorial videos play in YouTube’s own embedded player. The embedded player is loaded when you open a tutorial. YouTube may receive technical information when the player loads or plays. Once you play one, YouTube receives the request as it would on youtube.com, including your IP address and whatever its own cookies hold, all of it governed by the Google Privacy Policy. Trainr sends YouTube nothing about your profile, your plan or your logged sets, and never has.

Trainr asks you to sign in to nothing, so no Google account is connected to it. If you are signed in to Google in the player and want to review what that account has stored, Google’s security settings are where it is managed.

Advertising

The free tier shows one advertisement: a banner at the bottom of your weekly plan, served by Google AdMob. Trainr Pro has none, and while Pro is active no ad is requested at all.

To serve and measure that banner, Google’s advertising SDK receives the advertising identifier when permission and device settings allow it, your IP address (from which it infers an approximate location), and technical details of the device and the app, all governed by the Google Privacy Policy and described in how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. Trainr sends it nothing about your profile, your plan or your logged sets.

Where the law requires consent before ads can be personalised, in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, the app asks you first with Google’s own consent form, and you can change your answer at any time from Privacy options in the profile menu. On iOS the app also asks for App Tracking Transparency permission; declining prevents access to the IDFA and does not block the app. Ad delivery remains subject to your regional consent choices. Everywhere else you can turn off personalised ads in your phone’s own advertising settings.

Crash reports

Trainr uses Firebase Crashlytics so that when the app crashes, the developer finds out and can fix it. If a crash happens, the following is sent to Google:

  • The stack trace: which part of the code failed
  • What the app was doing at the time
  • Basic details about the device and the version of Android or iOS on it
  • A random identifier Crashlytics generates for your installation, used to tell one crash from the same crash happening twice

No part of your fitness profile is included in a crash report. Not your name, age, measurements, injuries, or anything you have logged. Crash reports say what broke, not who you are.

Development builds of the app do not send crash reports at all.

Health information

Your height, weight and any injuries you list are health-related. They are used for one thing: writing a workout plan that suits you and avoids what you asked it to avoid. They stay on your device. They are not sold, shared with advertisers, or used to build a profile of you.

The injuries question is the one optional question in onboarding; leave it blank and the plan is written without it.

Purchases

Apple or Google processes payments for Trainr Pro. Trainr uses RevenueCat to validate purchases, restore them on another installation and determine whether Pro is active. RevenueCat receives purchase and subscription information, an app-generated customer identifier, and technical information needed to provide this service. Trainr does not receive your payment-card details and does not send your fitness profile or logged sets to RevenueCat. See the RevenueCat Privacy Policy.

Permissions and limits

  • Trainr has no app accounts and does not ask for your email address.
  • It does not request access to contacts, photos, the microphone, camera or GPS.
  • It does not send your fitness profile or training history to advertisers.
  • Ads, purchases, exercise videos and crash reporting use the services described above. Those services can collect technical, usage and purchase information.

Android uses network permissions for these services. On iOS, Trainr requests tracking permission before allowing the advertising SDK to start. You can refuse this request and continue using the app. Permission can be changed in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. Some device or account restrictions prevent iOS from showing the request; Trainr respects that decision.

Children

Trainr is not intended for anyone under 13, and the app will not accept an age below 13.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Trainr removes its local profile and training database. It does not cancel a subscription or delete purchase records held by Apple, Google or RevenueCat. Manage subscriptions through the store where you purchased them. You can also clear it without uninstalling: on Android, Settings → Apps → Trainr → Storage → Clear storage; on iOS, delete the app, which removes its data with it.

Not medical advice

Plans are written by the app, not by a doctor or a trainer, and are not medical advice. Check with a doctor before you start, and stop if something hurts.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the history is public in this repository.
